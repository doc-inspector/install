PDF/A vs Regular PDF: Differences for Archiving, Audit, and Legal Work

Introduction

The Portable Document Format (PDF) has become the standard for sharing and storing documents due to its versatility and compatibility across various platforms. However, when it comes to archiving, auditing, and legal work, the regular PDF format may not be sufficient. This is where PDF/A comes into play, offering a standardized and self-contained format for long-term preservation of documents.

PDF/A, which stands for PDF/Archival, is a subset of the PDF format that ensures documents are archived in a way that they can be retrieved and rendered accurately over time, without relying on external resources. This makes it an essential format for organizations and individuals dealing with sensitive and critical documents.

Challenges in Document Archiving

One of the significant challenges in document archiving is ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the documents over time. Regular PDFs may contain links to external resources, such as fonts or images, which can become unavailable or corrupted, rendering the document unreadable. Moreover, regular PDFs can also contain metadata that may not be relevant or even compromising for archiving purposes.

PDF/A addresses these challenges by requiring that all fonts, images, and other resources be embedded within the document itself, making it self-contained and independent of external factors. This ensures that the document remains readable and intact, even after many years.

Verifying Document Compliance

Before archiving documents, it is crucial to verify their compliance with the PDF/A standard. This involves checking the document's structure, content, and metadata to ensure that it meets the requirements of the standard. This process can be time-consuming and requires specialized tools and expertise.

Recommended Workflow

To ensure that documents are properly archived and preserved, it is recommended to follow a standardized workflow. This involves creating documents in a format that is compatible with the PDF/A standard, verifying their compliance, and then archiving them in a secure and accessible manner.

Checklist for PDF/A Compliance

  • • Embed all fonts and images within the document
  • • Remove any external links or references
  • • Verify the document's structure and content
  • • Clean metadata and remove any sensitive information
  • • Use a compliant PDF/A creator or converter

Conclusion

In conclusion, PDF/A is a critical format for archiving, auditing, and legal work, offering a standardized and self-contained way to preserve documents over time.
